  • R6agree

  • R15agree

What to build next, for a medical student

Ordered by how often each comes up in MBBS exams and on the wards, and how much of the existing engine it reuses.

  1. 1

    Subacute combined degeneration and other tract combinations

    Vitamin B12 deficiency takes the posterior and lateral columns together: extensor plantars with absent ankle jerks when a neuropathy coexists. Every compartment it needs already exists; this is a preset and a frozen case.

    Reuses the cord engine. Very high-yield.

  2. 2

    The leg: lumbosacral plexus and nerves

    Common fibular (foot drop, with ankle inversion spared) against an L5 root (inversion weak). Also the tibial, sciatic, femoral and obturator nerves, and meralgia paraesthetica.

    Reuses the plexus engine. The most asked peripheral-nerve question after the hand.

  3. 3

    Visual pathways

    Optic nerve, chiasm, tract, radiation and cortex: monocular loss, bitemporal hemianopia, homonymous defects, quadrantanopias and macular sparing.

    New module. Examined everywhere.

  4. 4

    Eye movements

    Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (MLF), the one-and-a-half syndrome, and nuclear against fascicular third nerve lesions (S70 is already read).

    Extends the brainstem model.

  5. 5

    The facial nerve below the pons

    Bell's palsy along the nerve: taste, hyperacusis and lacrimation separate the sites. Also the cerebellopontine angle.

    Extends the face model.

  6. 6

    Cerebellum, basal ganglia and cortex

    Hemisphere against vermis signs, and aphasias. Also neglect and Gerstmann syndrome, and the PCA territory.

    Needs new signs in the examination.

  7. 7

    The standard spinal cord injury exam (ISNCSCI)

    Use its key sensory points and key muscles, the neurological level and the ASIA impairment grade, beside the classic charts.

    The form students meet in orthopaedics and emergency postings.

How to know it works

Passing tests shows the tool agrees with its sources. These studies would show that the sources are right, the answers are right, and students learn from it.

Clinical validation

  • Two independent reviewers fill in the review page; measure their agreement (Cohen's κ) and have a third settle disagreements.
  • Replace every “book reference pending” with a textbook page supplied by a reviewer, not guessed.
  • Re-read the cited StatPearls chapters once a year; they are revised.

Diagnostic accuracy

  • Collect 100 published or textbook case vignettes with a known lesion site. Report how often examination mode ranks the right answer first, and in its top three, by region.
  • Compare against a few clinicians given the same findings.

Question quality

  • Once students use it, score each generated case for difficulty (proportion right) and discrimination. Retire cases that strong students miss.
  • Check that distractors are chosen roughly evenly; an option nobody picks teaches nothing.

Learning

  • Pre-test, four weeks of use, post-test, then a delayed test, against a group reading the textbook chapter. At a college this needs ethics approval.
  • With enough answers, replace the fixed review intervals with a fitted scheduler such as FSRS.
