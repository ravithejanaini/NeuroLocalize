# P39 — Naming and explaining the parts

Written on 2026-10-06. The third of four phases that put on the stage what the model already
knows: after the cranial nerves (P37) and the visual pathway (P38), and before the sympathetic
pathway.

## What was missing

The stage draws a mark for every part the model reasons about above the cord — seventy-four a
side, and eleven of the visual pathway — and a lesion colours the ones it takes. But a mark said
nothing. A student could see that a dot in the pons turned red and not learn what the dot was,
what ran through it, or why the findings followed.

## What this phase adds

Tapping a mark, or choosing it by name from a list, opens a card with three things:

1. **What the model reports when that part alone is lost.** The engine is run on a lesion of that
   one part, and every reading of the examination that changes is listed, grouped where there are
   many — "Right · weak — 25 muscles" rather than twenty-five lines.
2. **The places that take it** — the arteries, syndromes and foramina among the model's lesions
   that include the part.
3. **What the model holds about it** — the claim of every row whose route passes through the
   part, word for word, with its tier and its sources.

## The rule it keeps

The card writes no fact of its own. Each statement is a row's reviewed claim; each finding is the
engine's; each place is the model's own list. A part the examination reads nothing from says so
rather than borrowing a sentence — ten parts do: the frontal eye fields and the neck areas of the
strips, whose findings have no slot in the examination, and the language areas and border zones
of the right hemisphere.

Tests hold this: every drawn part has a name unlike any other on its side; every statement is a
row of the knowledge base whose route does pass through the part, citing sources that exist; the
findings are empty exactly when the engine changes nothing; and the pyramid, the third nerve, the
chiasm and the optic tract give what their frozen cases give.

## Sources

None new, and none could be: a card can only show what is already cited.

## Limits

- Only the marks above the cord and on the visual pathway can be named. The cord's columns, the
  roots, the plexus and the nerves of the limbs cannot yet, nor can the end organs of the cranial
  nerves.
- The findings are for the part lost completely and alone, on the timepoint chosen. Real lesions
  seldom take one part.
- The wording of a finding is the examination's own label, which reads stiffly in places
  ("this eye does not adduct — present").
- A tap takes the nearest mark within sixteen pixels, whether or not something is drawn in front
  of it; the list by name is the sure way to a crowded part.
- No mutation run: nothing the engine reads changed.
