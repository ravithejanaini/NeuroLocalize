# P30 — The cavernous sinus, and the face by trigeminal division

Written on 2026-09-27 before any P30 code. Sources were read in full in the browser (NCBI
Bookshelf); every quotation below is verbatim. This answers C76 and R57.

## Why

A lesion of several ocular nerves at once on one side, with a numb forehead, is taught as the
cavernous sinus. The model could not say it: its face was one sensory field per side, so it could
not spare the jaw, and its oculosympathetic route stopped at the brainstem.

## What each source states

**The contents of the sinus** (S165, S166). S165: "The nerves of the cavernous sinus are the
oculomotor nerve (CN III), trochlear nerve (CN IV), ophthalmic nerve (V1), maxillary nerve (V2),
abducens nerve (CN VI), and the sympathetic plexus around the internal carotid artery." S166: "The
blood-filled space contains the internal carotid artery (carotid siphon) surrounded by sympathetic
fibers, as well as cranial nerves (CNs). In particular, CN III (oculomotor nerve), CN IV (trochlear
nerve), and 2 branches of CN V (trigeminal nerve), CN V1 (ophthalmic) and CN V2 (maxillary), pass
through this blood-filled space".

**What a whole-sinus lesion does** (S165, S166, S70). S165: "A severe lesion involving the entire
sinus will present with total ophthalmoplegia, due to CN III, IV, and VI injury"; "Horner’s syndrome
occurs when the sympathetic plexus around the internal carotid is damaged"; "When CN V1 and CN V2
are involved, sensory loss in the face, scalp, maxilla, nasal cavity, sinuses, and palate occurs."
S166: "It presents as proptosis, chemosis, ophthalmoplegia, Horner syndrome or trigeminal sensory
loss." S70: "The combination of a unilateral Horner syndrome and sixth nerve palsy also localizes to
the cavernous sinus".

**The three divisions** (S122). "The nerve's 3 main branches are the ophthalmic (V1), maxillary
(V2), and mandibular (V3) nerves." V1: "CN V1 travels lateral to the abducens nerve within the
cavernous sinus' lateral wall"; it "is responsible for the face and skull's sensory innervation
superior to the palpebral fissure". V2: "The nerve travels inferior to CN V1 within the cavernous
sinus' lateral wall"; "the territory inferior to the orbits and superior to the mouth". V3: "The
nerve exits the skull base through the foramen ovale"; it "receives sensory information from the
lower facial third, mouth floor, jaw, and tongue", and it is "the trigeminal nerve's only branch
with both sensory and motor components".

## Design

- The face is felt in three divisions per side: V1 (forehead), V2 (cheek), V3 (jaw). Every existing
  route to the face — the nuclei, VPM, the cortex — serves all three, so every earlier case reads
  the same; the whole-face finding is the worst of the three.
- One new part: the ophthalmic and maxillary divisions in the lateral wall of the sinus, serving V1
  and V2 on their own side. V3 is not in it: S165 and S166 list the sinus's nerves without it, and
  S122 sends it out through the foramen ovale. That the jaw is spared is therefore composed, not
  stated by one source.
- One new part on the oculosympathetic route: the sympathetic plexus on the carotid in the sinus.
- The cavernous sinus is a place taking the third, fourth and sixth nerves, V1 and V2 and the
  plexus, on one side. It ranks with the nerves, like the P29 places.
- The examination offers each division on each side, beside the whole-face row.

## Conflicts and limits

- **C77 — The pupil in a whole-sinus lesion.** S165: total ophthalmoplegia "accompanied by fixed and
  dilated pupils", yet the same lesion takes the sympathetic plexus, which gives Horner syndrome.
  The model has no pupil size (C45, C74); it asserts neither pupil, and reports Horner syndrome
  from the plexus as it does from the brainstem.
- **C78 — Partial sinus lesions.** S70 and S166 say the sinus "can produce" the palsies "in
  combination" — any subset. The model's place is the whole sinus; a partial lesion is not a place.
- **C79 — The onion skin.** The spinal trigeminal nucleus is laid out in concentric rings, not by
  division. The model reads a nuclear lesion as all three divisions, which is what the sources
  asserting ipsilateral facial loss say; the rings are not modelled.
