# P33 — The jugular foramen: the ninth, tenth and eleventh nerves

Written on 2026-10-01 before any P33 code. Sources were read in full in the browser (NCBI
Bookshelf); every quotation below is verbatim.

## Why

The lower cranial nerves leave the skull together, and a lesion where they do is a named
syndrome. The model had the palate only from inside the medulla (the nucleus ambiguus), where it
always comes with the lateral medullary signs, and had no sign at all for the ninth or the
eleventh nerve.

## What each source states

**The foramen and the syndrome** (S172, S174). S172: "Jugular foramen syndrome, also known as Vernet
syndrome, is characterized by dysfunction of cranial nerves IX, X, and XI as these structures
traverse the jugular foramen at the skull base." "The main structures that pass through this
foramen are the glossopharyngeal (IX), vagus (X), and spinal accessory (XI) nerves and the internal
jugular vein (IJV)." "The hypoglossal nerve (XII) does not traverse the jugular foramen (it exits
via the hypoglossal canal)." S174: injury there "could damage the 3 cranial nerves IX, X, or XI".

**The tenth nerve** (S172). "Vagal compression initially causes paralysis of the laryngeal muscles,
resulting in hoarseness and a nasal timbre. If the nerve is further compressed, it results in
unilateral paralysis of the soft palate and deviation of the uvula toward the normal side."

**The ninth nerve** (S172, S174, S175). S172: "Loss of sensation to the posterior ipsilateral aspect
of the tongue", "Dysphagia due to reduced pharyngeal constriction", "Loss of the ipsilateral gag
reflex". S174: "Cranial nerve IX damage may result in impaired taste in the posterior one-third of
the tongue, as well as cause dysphagia." S175: "General somatic afferent fibers (general sensory)
provide sensory innervation to the upper pharynx, the inner surface of the tympanic membrane, and
the posterior third of the tongue. Special sensory fibers also provide taste afferents from this
portion of the tongue." "It exits the skull via the jugular foramen".

**The eleventh nerve** (S172, S173, S174). S172: "Cranial nerve XI is a pure motor efferent supplying
the ipsilateral sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles." "This manifests as drooping of the
shoulder, difficulty abducting the ipsilateral arm, and contralateral rotation of the head due to
weakness of the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles." S173: "The SAN has a dual origin but
always exits through the jugular foramen"; "Contraction of a single SCM produces ipsilateral head
flexion with contralateral rotation"; "Trapezius function is tested by asking the patient to elevate
the shoulders with and without resistance. SCM function is evaluated by instructing the patient to
rotate the head to either side against resistance." S174: "The trapezius has many functions,
including shoulder shrugging"; "The sternocleidomastoid primarily acts to rotate the head to the
opposite side"; "Damage to cranial nerve XI primarily results in muscular pain in the shoulder and
neck, as well as weakness affecting the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius."

## Design

- Three new parts outside the brainstem, at the medulla: the glossopharyngeal, vagus and accessory
  nerves.
- Two new signs, each about one side. **The back of the tongue**: sensation and taste lost on the
  posterior third — three sources agree on it. **The eleventh nerve**: the shoulder droops and
  shrugs weakly on that side, and turning the head to the other side is weak (the sternocleidomastoid
  turns the head away from itself).
- The vagus nerve joins the route the nucleus ambiguus already feeds, so the palate is weak on its
  own side with the uvula pulled to the normal side.
- One place: the **jugular foramen**, taking all three nerves. It does not take the hypoglossal
  nerve, which S172 says leaves by its own canal; a strong tongue is asserted.

## Conflicts and limits

- **C87 — The gag reflex.** Only S172 gives "Loss of the ipsilateral gag reflex", and the reflex has
  two limbs — the ninth nerve in, the tenth out. It is not a finding of the model; the back of the
  tongue stands for the ninth nerve.
- **C88 — The eleventh nerve inside the cord and above it.** Its spinal root rises from C1 to C5
  (S173), and "These muscles also receive motor impulses from cervical nerves C1 to C4". The model's
  sign reads the nerve alone: a high cervical cord lesion and a hemisphere lesion give it no
  accessory sign. Not modelled, and no case asserts it.
- **C89 — The head's rotation.** S172 writes "contralateral rotation of the head" as a manifestation;
  S173 and S174 make the sternocleidomastoid the muscle that rotates the head to the opposite side,
  so its weakness weakens that turn. The sign is worded as the weak movement.
- **C90 — Hoarseness, dysphagia, parotid secretion and venous congestion** are not modelled.
- **C91 — Collet–Sicard and Villaret syndromes** (the twelfth nerve and the sympathetic chain added)
  are named in S173 but not described in a source read; not places.
