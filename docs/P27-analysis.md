# P27 — Four facts, searched in the primary literature

Asked on 2026-09-26 for a deeper search, outside StatPearls, of the four facts still open after
P26: the saddle's segments (R5, R54), the descending bladder pathway's place in the cord (R7), the
course of the tongue's cortical fibres (R25), and the root that carries hip adduction (R28). The
search used Europe PMC's index of the primary literature; each finding below was read verbatim
that day — in full where the paper is open, and marked **abstract only** where it is not. Search
summaries were used only to find papers.

Two papers were found and not used: Nathan and Smith's 1958 paper on the micturition pathway
(PMC497316), whose open copy is a scan with no readable text; and Schirmer et al.'s 2011
intraoperative myotome map, whose abstract gives no adductor figures.

## What changed

**R25 changed the model.** The model ran the tongue's cortical fibres on in the medullary
pyramid, so a medial medullary (Dejerine) lesion weakened both halves of the tongue: its own side
from the hypoglossal nucleus, and the other side through the pyramid. S156 and S157 put the
crossing at the pontomedullary junction, above the medulla, and S156 says a medullary lesion
reaches only the projections to its own side's nucleus. The route now ends at the pontine base.
The Dejerine case, which had left the other half of the tongue unasserted for exactly this reason,
now asserts it spared (A32); against the committed code it came back weak.

**R7, R28, R5 and R54 changed nothing.** S158 supports a bilateral descending bladder pathway —
which is why the model spares the bladder after a hemisection — but places it in the ventral and
lateral columns, where the model draws only the lateral one. S159 is evidence that L3 reaches the
adductors, not that adduction is L3; it stresses how much the map varies.
